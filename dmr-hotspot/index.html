Calibration

RX offset: done

Done with the WPSD calibration tool:

  1. Select DMR mode, click Start and wait for the status indicator to go green.
  2. On the radio, set TX Admit to Always temporarily and hold PTT on the normal hotspot channel (not VFO).
  3. Step the offset in 100 Hz, then 50 Hz, then 25 Hz steps, chasing the lowest BER.
  4. Run long enough to check the best offset doesn't drift as the board warms up.
  5. Save Offset, Stop, then set TX Admit back.

Result: BER 0.09% at RXOffset −75 Hz. DMR is comfortable up to about 1%.

TX offset and deviation: open

The calibration tool uses the radio as the signal source, so it only measures RX. TXOffset was set to −75 on the assumption that both ADF7021s share the same TCXO and the two frequencies are close, and DMRTXLevel was trialled at 45. Neither is a measurement. Proper TX calibration needs an RTL-SDR (or a spectrum analyser) to check the hotspot's actual transmitted frequency and deviation.

Troubleshooting log

Issue A: phantom TX lock-up (workaround in place)

  • Symptom: the dashboard freezes on TX: DMR, the radio shows "link timeout" on PTT, and network traffic on the dashboard doesn't reach the radio. It receives fine for days and tends to happen around transmitting.
  • Diagnosis: the modem reports it is transmitting and never reports completion, so it is stuck at the STM32/firmware level.
  • Workaround: sudo systemctl restart mmdvmhost restores it every time. Keep the WPSD Services Watchdog enabled for automatic recovery; a systemd timer that checks the modem and restarts it is a fallback.
  • Suspected root cause: power (see below).

Issue B: carrier but no DMR decode on the handheld (open)

  • Symptom: the radio's RX LED and signal meter respond to the hotspot's 438.300 MHz transmission, but no audio or callsign is decoded. Seen on TG93, TG214, TG222, TG255 and Parrot TG9990, and later as intermittent drop-outs.
  • Key point: carrier detect and the S-meter only prove RF is present, not that the modulation can be decoded.
  • Tried: TXOffset −75 and DMRTXLevel 45, with no fix.
  • Next: measure TX frequency and deviation with an RTL-SDR, and correlate drop-outs with BER on the dashboard and with under-voltage events.

Resolved

  • The SVC LED flashing red is the normal firmware heartbeat, not a fault. An earlier forum-based diagnosis was wrong.

Power investigation

CheckResult
vcgencmd get_throttled0x50000: under-voltage and throttling have occurred since boot
After a cable swapStill 0x50000
Live check0x50005: under-voltage and throttling currently active
Supply5 V / 3.5 A wall charger; both cables of unknown gauge

Hypothesis: load spikes (at boot, and from the dual hat while transmitting) cause brief voltage sags at the Pi. These glitch the UART to the STM32 and leave the modem wedged, which would explain Issue A and may contribute to Issue B.

Reading get_throttled: bits 0–3 mean "happening now" (0x1 under-voltage, 0x2 frequency capped, 0x4 throttled, 0x8 soft temperature limit); bits 16–19 mean "has happened since boot". The flags only clear on reboot. Watch them live with watch -n 1 vcgencmd get_throttled.

Fix options, in order of effort:

  1. A short Micro-USB cable with a verified 20–24 AWG power pair (cheap cables are typically 28–30 AWG).
  2. Measure the voltage at the Pi under load with a multimeter (test points, or GPIO pins 2/4 to 6).
  3. GPIO power injection: feed regulated 5 V straight to header pins 2/4 (5 V) and 6 (GND), bypassing the Micro-USB connector and cable losses. This also bypasses the Pi's input polyfuse, so use only a clean, verified 5 V supply.

Mobile version (12 V vehicle power)

A mobile version of the Pi 3B and MMDVM hat, powered from a vehicle's 12 V system, has also been explored.

  • Didn't work: LM2596 and XL4015 buck modules, and a homebrew LM317 regulator. They caused RF interference in the receiver or board resets.
  • Planned: vehicle 12 V → fuse → LC/ferrite input filter → Pololu D24V50F5 (or equivalent) synchronous 5 V buck → LC/ferrite output filter → Pi 3B and hat.
  • No-solder alternative: a quality USB car charger with clip-on ferrites on the cable.

Lessons learned

  • Carrier and S-meter response are not the same as a successful DMR decode. Verify the modulation.
  • Guessing DMRTXLevel and TXOffset isn't calibration. Measure with an SDR.
  • A clean get_throttled at one moment doesn't rule out power; the "since boot" bits and a live watch tell the real story.
  • Don't trust forum diagnoses of LED patterns without checking the firmware's actual behaviour.
